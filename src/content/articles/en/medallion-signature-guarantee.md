**If your form requires a Medallion Signature Guarantee, contact a participating financial institution.** It is a securities-transfer signature guarantee, [not a notarial service](#ref-fam). Notary Zhou does not provide Medallion guarantees, and a notarial stamp is not a substitute.

## Why notarization cannot replace it

The State Department describes a guarantee in which a financial institution stands behind the signature’s authenticity and [accepts liability for forgery](#ref-fam). A California acknowledgment identifies the signer; it [does not certify the document’s truthfulness, accuracy, or validity](#ref-acknowledgment). The services have different functions and responsibilities.

Read the exact wording on the form. “Notarization,” “signature certification,” and “Medallion Signature Guarantee” are not interchangeable requests for a stamp.

## Where to start

The SEC recommends asking a bank, credit union or brokerage you already use whether it [participates in a Medallion program and can assist you](#ref-investor). Participation is not universal, and a participating institution may decline to guarantee a noncustomer’s signature.

Before booking, ask:

1. Does this transaction require Medallion, or is another approved process available?
2. Which branch handles it, and is an existing account relationship or additional review required?
3. What identity, ownership or representative-authority documents are needed, and must I sign there?
4. Will its guarantee cover the transaction value, and what are the fee and processing time?

This is a question list, not a universal document checklist. Have the broker or transfer agent review the actual form before you travel.

## If you are outside the United States

SEC guidance points overseas investors toward [overseas branches of U.S. or Canadian financial institutions with which they do business](#ref-overseas). Availability is not guaranteed. If that route fails, ask the requesting transfer agent or issuer for assistance; do not replace its requirement with a local notarization on your own.

A U.S. embassy or consulate is not a Medallion provider. [Consular officers lack authority to issue these guarantees](#ref-fam), even though ordinary consular notarial services are a separate matter.

## Not every securities-related form requires Medallion

Do not assume that every financial form has the same requirement. TreasuryDirect, for example, says [some forms expressly allow a notary to certify a signature](#ref-treasury). Other forms require different certifying officials. Follow the current form and the receiving institution’s instructions.

If the recipient confirms that a separate document needs ordinary notarization, see [acknowledgment vs. jurat](/en/articles/acknowledgment-vs-jurat/). For language questions, see [certified and notarized translations](/en/articles/chinese-documents-translation/).

This guide helps readers find the appropriate institution; it is not an offer to arrange a securities transfer or obtain a Medallion guarantee.
