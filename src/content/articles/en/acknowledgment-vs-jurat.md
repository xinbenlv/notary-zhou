**In California, an acknowledgment confirms that you executed a document. A jurat also requires signing before the notary and an oath or affirmation about its contents.** Both involve personal appearance and identity verification. Convenience is not a reason to substitute one for the type the recipient requires. [Acknowledgment requirements](#ref-acknowledgment) · [Jurat requirements](#ref-jurat).

These are California rules. Confirm local certificate wording and procedures when the notarization takes place in another state or country.

## The practical comparison

| Requirement | Acknowledgment | Jurat |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Appear and establish identity | Required | Required |
| What you do | Acknowledge that you executed the document | Sign and swear or affirm to its contents |
| Sign before the appointment? | Permitted; acknowledge it in person | Sign in the notary’s presence |
| Notary guarantees the contents? | No | No |

The [acknowledgment statute](#ref-acknowledgment) and [jurat statute](#ref-jurat) establish the procedures and limits. California’s handbook explains [when signing beforehand is permitted](#ref-signing). With a jurat, the signer makes the sworn statement; the notary does not investigate or endorse it.

## What if I already signed?

For an acknowledgment, [a prior signature is permitted](#ref-signing), but you still need to appear and acknowledge execution. Sending the document with a relative does not replace your appearance.

For a jurat, leave space to sign during the appointment and follow the notary’s instructions for the oath or affirmation. If you already signed, ask the notary how to complete the signing requirement. Do not erase parts of the document or ask for a past notarial date.

For example, a translator whose certification requires an acknowledgment may appear with a previously signed statement. If it requires a jurat, the translator must sign before the notary and take an oath or affirmation. See [certified vs. notarized translations](/en/articles/chinese-documents-translation/).

## Who chooses the certificate?

Start with the recipient’s certificate or written instructions. If those are missing, ask the recipient or your attorney what is required. California’s handbook prohibits a notary from [selecting legal documents or giving legal advice](#ref-legal-choice). Explaining the procedural difference does not authorize the notary to choose the legal purpose for you.

A heading such as “affidavit” or “power of attorney” is not a reason to skip the recipient’s instructions. A useful request is: “Please confirm whether you require an acknowledgment or jurat and send the certificate requirements.”

## Neither establishes that a document is valid

Both California certificates exclude verification of the document’s truthfulness, accuracy or validity. [Acknowledgment notice](#ref-acknowledgment) · [Jurat notice](#ref-jurat). A completed notarization is not a legal review of a contract, a representative’s authority, or requirements for use abroad.
