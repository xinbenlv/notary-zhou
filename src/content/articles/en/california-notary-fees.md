**California caps an acknowledgment at $15 per signature. Administering an oath or affirmation to one person and executing the jurat is also capped at $15.** These are [statutory maximums](#ref-fees), not a universal price per document or a nationwide U.S. rate.

All amounts below are U.S. dollars; rules were checked September 15, 2026. A quote should distinguish the notarial act, other services and any mandatory fee waiver.

## The fee limits

The following limits come from [California Government Code §8211](#ref-fees).

| Service | Maximum and charging unit |
| --- | --- |
| Acknowledgment or statutory proof of execution | $15 per signature, including seal and certificate |
| Oath or affirmation to one person and execution of a jurat | $15, including seal |
| Authorized certification of a power-of-attorney copy | $15 per copy |
| Deposition services | $30, plus $7 for the witness’s oath and $7 for the certificate |
| Notarizing signatures on vote-by-mail identification envelopes or other voting materials | $0 |
| Specified U.S. military veteran benefit applications or claims | $0 |

A maximum is not a requirement to charge that amount. California’s handbook permits [lower charges or no charge](#ref-handbook-fees).

## Two people sign one document: how much?

For acknowledgments, two signatures each requiring notarization would cost at most **2 × $15 = $30**. One signer obtaining an acknowledgment on each of three documents would cost at most **3 × $15 = $45**. These illustrate the [statutory charging unit](#ref-fees); they are not a service quote.

Count the required acts, not pages or every signature printed on the form. Confirm which signatures need which act. The jurat provision refers to an oath or affirmation to one person and execution of the jurat; do not treat every notarial service as an acknowledgment charged by signature. See [acknowledgment vs. jurat](/en/articles/acknowledgment-vs-jurat/).

## Ask separately about mobile travel charges

California’s handbook requires the notarial fee to be recorded and says [travel fees should be identified separately](#ref-travel). Ask for the notarial charge, travel charge, other agreed services, total and cancellation terms before a trip begins.

The $15 acknowledgment limit does not mean an entire mobile appointment costs $15. Equally, “travel” is not a universal exception to mandatory free-service rules. See the [homepage](/en/#book) for current appointment arrangements; this guide does not promise a fixed mobile total or an office appointment.

## Free services have specific boundaries

Voting-material and veteran-benefit exceptions appear in [§8211(d) and (f)](#ref-fees). For specified veteran benefit matters, §6107 also prohibits [fees or compensation for related services](#ref-veterans). Check the full exemption before quoting; do not assume a separate travel charge is allowed.

The handbook lists additional exceptions involving specified military-reservation appointments, certain public-employee pension services and election nomination documents. [Each has conditions](#ref-handbook-fees); veteran status alone does not make every unrelated document free.

## If a charge seems wrong

Retain the quote, receipt and relevant documents, and ask for an explanation of each charge. Suspected violations of a notary’s duties may be [reported to the California Secretary of State](#ref-complaint). Its process concerns discipline; recovery of money or invalidation of documents requires a separate appropriate remedy.
