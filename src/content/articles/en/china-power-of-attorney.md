**If you are in the United States and need someone to handle a property, banking or other matter in mainland China, first have the actual receiving institution confirm the document and authority it requires.** Notarization and an Apostille address only part of the process. They do not add missing powers or guarantee acceptance.

This guide uses a California signing as its example. Other U.S. states have their own requirements; documents signed in Canada, the United Kingdom or another country need the appropriate local authority. This guide concerns mainland China. Requirements for Taiwan, Hong Kong and Macao must be checked separately.

## Work backward from the recipient

![Concept illustration: notarization, authentication and delivery are separate steps in moving a document; none substitutes for the recipient's review of its contents](/images/articles/china-power-of-attorney/01-the-chain.jpg)

| Step | What it addresses | What it does not replace |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Recipient or lawyer confirms the text | The transaction, powers and supporting evidence | Proper execution |
| Local notarization | Required identity checks, acknowledgment or oath | Legal review of the transaction or powers |
| Apostille authority | The public official's signature, capacity and seal | Verification of the document's contents |
| Submission to the recipient | Delivery of the required originals, translations and other material | Approval of the transaction |

The Chinese Consulate General in San Francisco directs applicants to [prepare a power of attorney according to the receiving institution's requirements](#ref-sf-text). Its Apostille notice also advises checking format, content, time limits and translation before arranging the paperwork. [An Apostille does not guarantee that the recipient will accept it](#ref-china-apostille).

Ask the recipient whether it has a prescribed format; which acts the agent must be authorized to perform; whether other rights holders are involved; how many originals and supporting records it needs; whether translation is required; and which notarial act it accepts. Have a lawyer familiar with the transaction review powers involving property sales, inheritance or asset disposal. California notaries [may not draft or select legal documents for customers](#ref-ca-legal).

## Why the Apostille route no longer includes Chinese consular legalization

The consulate's formal notice states that, from **November 7, 2023**, U.S. public documents covered by the Apostille Convention [can be used in mainland China after a U.S. Apostille, without U.S. and Chinese consular legalization](#ref-china-apostille). An old checklist that automatically adds legalization at a Chinese consulate should therefore not be followed unchanged.

This is not a rule that any stamped power of attorney becomes valid. The California Secretary of State says an [Apostille does not validate the contents](#ref-ca-apostille). Whether the powers are sufficient and the transaction requirements are met remains a separate question.

For a California notarization, consult the California Secretary of State's instructions; its role is to authenticate California public officials' signatures. Neither your California address nor a recipient in China makes California the authority for every document in your file. See the [Apostille guide](/en/articles/apostille-for-china/) for how document origin affects the route.

## The California appointment: do not assume every power of attorney needs the same act

**Have the recipient or a lawyer establish the required notarial type**, then have the notary carry out the applicable formalities.

- **Acknowledgment:** The signer must personally appear and acknowledge execution. The document may already have been signed.
- **Jurat:** The signer must personally appear, sign in the notary's presence, and take an oath or affirmation.

The distinction is set out in the [California Notary Public Handbook](#ref-ca-acts). Neither certificate verifies the document's truth, accuracy or validity. If you are unsure when to sign, clarify before the appointment; do not switch notarial types merely to save time.

Powers of attorney also involve a [thumbprint in the notary's journal](#ref-ca-journal), normally the right thumb, with statutory alternatives or a recorded explanation when it cannot be supplied. That journal entry does not separately certify every attachment to the power of attorney.

For a California Apostille, [the notarization may be in English while the body uses another language](#ref-ca-language). The notary must also be able to communicate directly with the signer; an interpreter should not substitute for that communication. [California's handbook explains the language requirement](#ref-ca-communication).

## Originals, fees and timing

California's [mail application checklist](#ref-ca-request) calls for the original notarized or certified document, a cover sheet identifying the country of use, payment of **US$20 per Apostille**, and a self-addressed return envelope. An ordinary photocopy does not meet that original-document requirement. Mailing addresses differ by delivery service; use the current official instructions.

In-person requests have an additional [US$6 special-handling charge for each different public official's signature](#ref-ca-fees). These are Secretary of State charges. They do not include notarization, travel, translation or international shipping, so they should not be presented as an all-inclusive price.

Mail processing depends on the queue. The official page describes processing in receipt order and links the current dates. Check the available method and processing status before sending the document; courier delivery time is not government processing time. Keep a full scan for your records, while confirming with the recipient whether it requires the original or will accept a scan.

## Chinese consular notarization remains a separate service

Ending consular legalization did not end consular notarization. The San Francisco consulate still lists powers of attorney among its notarial services, but [eligibility generally concerns Chinese citizens residing in its district, and the service has subject-matter exclusions](#ref-sf-scope).

Its guidance excludes notarization involving purchase or disposal of real property and other major assets. Examples include property sales, mortgages, receipt of sale proceeds, waiver of property inheritance and disposal of substantial bank deposits. [Those exclusions should not be restated as a ban on every banking or inheritance-related document](#ref-sf-scope). Check the actual transaction against the competent consulate's current rules.

If the principal's name differs from the ownership records, resolve the [same person affidavit or official name-change evidence](/en/articles/same-person-affidavit/) question early. If marital-status evidence is requested, use the separate [single status affidavit guide](/en/articles/single-status-affidavit/) rather than merging different purposes into one statement.
