**Obtain the right recorded marriage certificate before applying for an apostille.** A marriage license authorizes marriage; a marriage certificate records that the marriage happened. The word “marriage” on a document is not enough to identify what a foreign institution needs.[License and certificate distinction](#ref-usa-marriage)

Apostilles for state or local marriage records generally come from the [designated authority for the issuing state or jurisdiction](#ref-us-authorities). Your current address does not change where the record belongs.

## 1. Find the record custodian

USAGov directs people married in the United States to the [marriage state's vital records office](#ref-usa-marriage) for certified-copy instructions. The specific county office depends on local rules and custody of the record; do not assume any county can print your certificate.

In Santa Clara County, for example, the record is held in the [county that issued the marriage license](#ref-scc-record). Its instructions explain that the completed license becomes a marriage certificate after recording. If the ceremony was recent, confirm recording before scheduling authentication and delivery.

Have the couple's names, marriage date and license-issuing county ready. If the record contains an error, ask the custodian about correction rather than altering the certified copy yourself.

## 2. Order a copy appropriate for the purpose

**Santa Clara County, California, example:** the county distinguishes a Certified Authorized Copy from a Certified Informational Copy. An informational copy is [generally not accepted as proof of marriage](#ref-scc-copies), so do not substitute it merely because it is easier to obtain.

For a confidential marriage, the county limits a certified-copy request to [a person named on the certificate or someone with an authorizing court order](#ref-scc-confidential). Those rules differ from access to standard marriage records. Check other jurisdictions separately.

A related distinction matters when ordering by mail or online: the county requires the applicant's [statement to be signed before a notary](#ref-scc-application). That notarization concerns the application. It is not a notary making a certified copy of the marriage record; California notaries [cannot perform that certification](#ref-ca-notary).

## 3. Use the issuing jurisdiction's apostille process

Once you have the appropriate copy, check its official signature and title against the issuing state's instructions. California applicants should use the [Secretary of State's application page](#ref-ca-request). For another jurisdiction, find its office in the [HCCH authority directory](#ref-us-authorities).

Keep three sets of requirements separate:

1. **Record custodian:** which copy it issues and who may obtain it.
2. **Authentication authority:** which signatures it accepts and how to submit and pay.
3. **Recipient:** acceptable format, number of copies and translation requirements.

This is a preparation checklist, not a uniform national timeline. The [apostille overview](/en/articles/apostille-for-china/) explains the broader distinctions.

## Does a U.S. marriage certificate for mainland China still need legalization?

Since November 7, 2023, covered U.S. public documents follow the [U.S. apostille route](#ref-china-route) instead of the former consular legalization process. The recipient still decides whether the material meets the requirements of the particular application. An apostille [does not guarantee acceptance](#ref-china-acceptance).

Ask a concrete question before applying: “Will a certified marriage record from this U.S. state and county, with a U.S. apostille, meet this application's requirements? What translation, number of copies and issue date do you require?” Identifying the authority and purpose is more useful than asking whether “American marriage notarization” is sufficient.

This guide does not determine whether a marriage has substantive legal effect in the destination. For a dispute over marital status or registration, consult the receiving authority or an appropriately qualified legal professional. A marriage record issued outside the United States also cannot automatically use the U.S. state process.
