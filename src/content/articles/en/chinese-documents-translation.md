**Find out whether the recipient wants a translation or a notarized signature.** A certified translation concerns the translator’s certification. California notarization of that certification [does not establish that the translation is accurate](#ref-certificate). This guide separates USCIS requirements from California notarial rules; it is not a worldwide standard.

## What USCIS requires

USCIS requires a [full English translation](#ref-uscis) of foreign-language supporting documents. The translator must certify completeness and accuracy, and competence to translate from the source language into English. Its “Document Translations” provision requires translator certification without listing notarization as part of that requirement. Check the particular form instructions and any evidence request too.

Ask whether the translation is complete, the certification identifies the document, and the translator has made the required statements. A notarial stamp does not remedy missing text or an absent translator certification.

## When the recipient asks for a notarized translation

Ask whether it means **notarization of the translator’s signature on a statement of accuracy**, and which notarial certificate it requires. California acknowledgment and jurat certificates [do not establish the truthfulness, accuracy, or validity of the attached document](#ref-certificate). Translation quality remains the subject of the translator’s statement.

The person whose signature is being notarized must appear. A customer cannot bring an absent translator’s signed statement and stand in for that translator. However, an existing signature is not automatically disqualifying:

- **Acknowledgment:** the translator [may sign beforehand, then appear and acknowledge execution](#ref-signing).
- **Jurat:** the translator must [sign before the notary and take an oath or affirmation](#ref-signing).

Speaking Chinese does not mean that a notary has been hired to translate. Confirm any separate translation service explicitly; a limit on notarial authority is not a blanket prohibition on a person doing translation work separately.

## Can the original Chinese document be notarized in California?

A notary may notarize a signature on a foreign-language document. The document must not appear incomplete, and the notary must communicate directly with the signer. California’s handbook says [an interpreter should not be used for that communication](#ref-language); if communication is not possible, seek a notary who speaks the signer’s language.

This does not establish acceptance abroad. California’s apostille FAQ allows a document’s body to be in another language when [the notarization is in English](#ref-apostille-language). An apostille does not remove a recipient’s separate translation requirements.

## Prepare in this order

Obtain the recipient’s written requirements, prepare the original, translation and translator certification, then arrange an appearance by the appropriate signer if notarization is required. For foreign use, also confirm authentication and translation requirements before mailing documents.
